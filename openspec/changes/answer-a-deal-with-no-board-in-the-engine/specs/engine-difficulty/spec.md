## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: A deal is bounded by one deadline, the same in every game

The engine's deal SHALL arm one wall-clock deadline, of the same length in
every game and at every size, and a retry loop that states no count SHALL give
up when it passes and not at a count of tries. A generator SHALL NOT state a
budget for its deal. The length is weighed between two waits: a size with no
board answers after the whole of it, and a tier found once in `m` seconds runs
it out with chance `e^(-length/m)`.

#### Scenario: A size with no board is answered after the same wait in any game

- **WHEN** the app deals a type whose generator never finds a board, in a game
  where a try takes microseconds and in one where it takes milliseconds
- **THEN** each is answered with the sentence for a deal that found no board
  when the deadline passes
- **AND** neither generator states how many tries it may make

#### Scenario: A board rarer than the house count is dealt

- **WHEN** the app deals a type whose board is found once in more tries than
  a generator called directly is allowed, and within the deadline
- **THEN** the board is dealt

#### Scenario: The deadline ends a deal and never chooses its board

- **WHEN** a seed's deal ends with a board under one deadline
- **THEN** it ends with the same board under any longer one, and with the same
  board where the generator is called directly and its count allows
- **AND** a deadline that passes first deals no board, and never an earlier one

### Requirement: A generator called directly is bounded by a count

Where no deal armed a deadline, a retry loop that states no count SHALL give
up after the house count of tries, and SHALL read no clock, so that what a
test or a census is answered does not depend on the load on the machine. A
test that deals a board known to be rarer than that SHALL raise the count for
that deal, and SHALL NOT arm a deadline to find it.

#### Scenario: A test's deal does not fail for a busy machine

- **WHEN** a test calls a generator directly on a machine too busy to finish
  the deal within the app's deadline
- **THEN** the deal ends as it does on an idle machine: with its board, or at
  the house count of tries

#### Scenario: A test deals a cell known to be rare

- **WHEN** a test pins that a size deals its tier, and a board there is found
  once in more tries than the house count
- **THEN** the test raises the count for that deal, in one place the engine's
  test helpers share, and the game's generator states no number for it

### Requirement: A count on a retry loop is the algorithm's and not a budget

A retry loop SHALL state a count of its own only where the number is part of
the algorithm: a stage that hands over to the next when it is spent, or a
maximum the board itself sets. Such a count SHALL apply whether or not a
deadline is armed, and the loop SHALL give up at the deadline as well. A count
sized from how rare a board is, or from how long giving up takes, is a budget
and SHALL NOT be stated.

#### Scenario: A stage hands over at its count under a deadline

- **WHEN** a generator draws its boards in stages, and a stage's run-out is
  caught and answered by drawing the stage before it again (Loopy's boards on
  one patch of an aperiodic tiling)
- **THEN** the stage gives up at its count while the app's deadline is armed,
  as it does when the generator is called directly

#### Scenario: A new generator's deal-again loop states no number

- **WHEN** a new generator deals a whole board again until one is good
- **THEN** its guard is given a label and no count

### Requirement: The tier gate's cost is measured in seconds, by its worst case

The cost of the tier gate's extra solver run, and of the rejections it causes,
SHALL be measured in seconds a board, by the worst case over repeated seeds
and not the median: a deal that a legal seed can carry past the deadline turns
a rare puzzle into a visible failure.

#### Scenario: A gate's cost is weighed

- **WHEN** the time a gated generator takes over a board is measured over
  repeated seeds
- **THEN** the largest seen is what is set against the deal's deadline, and
  not the median

### Requirement: A rare tier is dealt under the deadline

A tier that is merely *rare* SHALL NOT be refused where the deal usually finds
it within the deadline: it is generated by retrying, under the engine's
deadline, and the game states no bound for it. Where the retry takes seconds a
board, the pair SHOULD still be dealt, since the app deals the next board
ahead (`dealing`, "The next board is dealt ahead and kept") and the wait is
the first board's alone.

#### Scenario: A rare tier that deals promptly is dealt

- **WHEN** a size has boards at a tier that the generator finds only after
  many tries, each try taking a small fraction of a second in total
- **THEN** the app's deal keeps trying and deals a board that needs that tier
- **AND** the generator states no count of tries for it

#### Scenario: A rare tier that takes seconds is dealt

- **WHEN** a size has boards at a tier found once in a measured number of
  tries, taking seconds a board
- **THEN** parameter validation accepts the pair when a board is to be dealt
- **AND** the deal runs out only where the deadline passes first

## REMOVED Requirements

### Requirement: The tier gate's cost is measured by its worst case

**Reason**: It had a generator's retry bound set above the worst count of
tries measured, and no generator states one. The cost is weighed in seconds
against the deal's deadline.
**Migration**: "The tier gate's cost is measured in seconds, by its worst
case", added by this change.

### Requirement: A rare tier is dealt by retrying

**Reason**: It had a retry bound sized to the rate measured for the pair, and
no generator states one.
**Migration**: "A rare tier is dealt under the deadline", added by this
change.

## MODIFIED Requirements

### Requirement: A tier too rare to deal says so

A size and tier that is rare, and not known to be absent, SHALL be refused, in
place of being dealt, only where the first wait is longer than a player would
sit through once, or where a long count found no board at all. Such a refusal
SHALL say that such boards are too rare to deal and SHALL NOT say that none
exists.

#### Scenario: A tier too rare to deal says so

- **WHEN** a size has boards at a tier but a count found none, or finding the
  first takes longer than a player would wait, and parameter validation
  rejects the pair when a board is to be dealt
- **THEN** the message says such boards are too rare to deal, not that there
  are none

### Requirement: A generator that runs out of tries is answered, not thrown

Where a deal finds no board before its deadline, or a count of the
generator's own runs out, the engine SHALL report it to the caller as a sentence a
player can be shown, and SHALL leave the board in play, and the parameters it
was dealt at, as they were. Either says that no board was found, which is an
answer about the parameters and not a fault. The sentence SHALL say that the
tier may be rare or may be absent, and SHALL NOT assert either, since the
generator cannot tell them apart.

#### Scenario: A deal that finds no board keeps the one in play

- **WHEN** a type is chosen whose generator finds no board before the deadline
- **THEN** the player is shown a sentence saying no puzzle of that type was
  found
- **AND** the board that was on screen is still in play
- **AND** the type shown as chosen is that board's

#### Scenario: A seed that finds no board is refused like any other id

- **WHEN** a game ID carrying a seed is loaded and its generator finds no
  board before the deadline
- **THEN** loading returns the same sentence, as it returns any other refusal

### Requirement: Every generate-until-success loop is bounded by the shared retry limit

Every generate-until-success retry loop in a game generator SHALL be finitely
bounded, and the bound SHALL come from the shared `engine/retry-limit.ts`
helper rather than a hand-rolled counter, so every loop reports failure the
same way: `RetryLimitExceeded`, naming the loop and the tries it made. A loop
whose only termination argument is probabilistic counts as unbounded. Fixpoint
solvers, and loops that terminate by a stated monotone-progress argument, are
exempt.

#### Scenario: A runaway generator fails fast instead of orphaning a worker

- **WHEN** a game generator's retry loop is given an input for which it never
  reaches success (a porting divergence, or params that admit no puzzle)
- **THEN** it throws `RetryLimitExceeded`, naming the loop, after a finite number
  of attempts rather than looping forever, so the worker returns control (and can
  be torn down) instead of becoming an uninterruptible orphan
