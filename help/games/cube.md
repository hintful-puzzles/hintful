# Cube

Roll the cube around the grid, picking up the {{pair:0}} squares on its
faces. Try to get all the {{pair:0}} squares on to the object at the same
time, in as few moves as possible.

## Controls

Use the arrow keys to roll the cube, or 8, 2, 4 and 6 on the numeric
keypad, or click the mouse where you want it to roll towards. After every roll, the grid square and cube
face that you brought into contact swap their colors, so that a
plain cube face can pick up a {{pair:0}} square, but a {{pair:0}} face rolled
on to a plain square puts it down again.

On the triangular grids a triangle has one flat side, above or below it, and two sloping ones. Up or Down, whichever points at the flat side, rolls the solid across it, and Left and Right roll it across the sloping sides. The diagonal keys of the numeric keypad (7, 9, 1 and 3) make the same three rolls: the two that lean toward the flat side both cross it, and each of the other two crosses the sloping side it leans toward.

When you have mastered the cube, use the Type menu to select other
regular solids!

## Hints

**Hint** shows the next roll rather than making it. No roll in Cube is
forced, so the hint plans a line of rolls that gathers every
{{pair:0}} square onto the solid, and shows it one roll at a time. Each
run of rolls ends by picking a square up, and the rolls on the way say
which square that is. On the cube and the tetrahedron the line is a
shortest one; on the octahedron and the icosahedron it is a good one,
not always the shortest.

{{hint-marks}}

Two things catch everyone out, and the hint says them where they come
up. A square is picked up only by a plain face, so rolling straight on
to a {{pair:0}} square can land a {{pair:0}} face on it and pick nothing
up: then the hint goes round to come at it another way. And a
{{pair:0}} face rolled on to a plain square leaves its paint there.
Late in the game, with most faces {{pair:0}}, that is often the only
way on: the hint says when no square can be picked up without putting
some paint down first, and when the paint it leaves is picked up again
by another face. That is how paint moves from one face to another.

A {{pair:0}} face rolled on to a {{pair:0}} square changes nothing, so
the hint's line may cross {{pair:0}} squares on its way.

On a custom grid much larger than the ones on the Type menu, the search
can run out before it finds a line, and the hint says so.
